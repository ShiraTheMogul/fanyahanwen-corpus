# Overview

Shang (商方) is the earliest extant state to exist in the Chinese region, and the earliest point in history where the Han script can be seen. The earliest oracle bones date back to around 1230 BCE, in the period of King Wu Ding. They were a highly religious society with a wealth of bronzeware, carved jade, and chariot-based warfare. 

# History

The earliest traceable individual of the Shang dynasty through contemporary sources appears to be Wang Hai 王亥, who is venerated in Oracle bones. His exact birth date is unknown, but Zhou dynasty texts mention him dying during the legendary Xia dynasty after negotiations for trade with the Youyi tribe broke down, and he was killed for his cattle. His mythical grandfather, Xiang Tu 相土, was said to have created horse-drawn chariots, allowing for his successors, including Wang Hai's father, Ming 冥, to continue his legacy. Wang Hai's son, Shang Jia 上甲, avenged Wang Hai by obliterating the Youyi 有易. This has led to Wang Hai being seen as the father of Chinese merchants today.

The Shang become clearer once we arrive at Wu Ding, whose palace at Yin 殷 was discovered in the 19th Century. The palace was filled to the brim with Oracle bones from his period onwards, and various tombs of Shang officials have validated - but also significantly challenged - Zhou-Qin and Han dynasty narratives. Wu Ding was a prolific diviner and would actively participate in Shang religious culture. Chariots, jade weapons, poleaxes, and spears were popular weapons in his military, allowing for rapid expansion, including the defeat of the Guifang 鬼方 and adopting them into his empire. 

As time went on, Shang seemingly declined. Reasons for this are unclear, but Di Xin 帝辛, the last ruler, is known to have gone to war in the east with the Renfang 人方. During this time, around c.1046 BCE, the Zhoufang 周方 attacked, overwhelmed, and defeated Shang at their capital. Thereafter, the Western Zhou dynasty 西周朝 was founded.

The Zhou dynasty had written numerous narratives about the Shang conquest and its culture, though these texts all date to around 500 BCE, half a millenium later. It is proven that there were extant records: Sima Qian 司馬遷, a Han dynasty author, was largely correct about the names of Shang monarchs, for example. However, narratives about the Shang rulers became progressively more negative over time, until the later monarchs were seen as savage monsters. 

# Culture

Shang held a highly religious aristocracy, who would engage in ancestor veneration under the belief they would receive divine protection, good harvests, and avoid troubles such as toothache and nightmares. They primarily ate various varieties of millet, grain, and rice, as well as the meat of animals. Bronze and jade remnants show they made cooking implements, including cookers, out of these materials. 

The Shang worshipped Four "Powers": Di 帝, Tu 土, He 河, and Yue 岳, corresponding to what is now Shangdi, the Earth, the Yellow River, and Mt. Song. There were then Six Spirits, predynastic leaders of Shang: Shang Jia 上甲, Bao Yi 匚乙, Bao Bing 匚丙, Bao Ding 匚丁, Shi Ren 示壬, and Shi Gui 示癸. Shi Gui's son, Da Yi 大乙, is identified with Tang of Shang, who was said in Zhou dynasty texts to have defeated the legendary Xia dynasty to begin the Shang dynasty. However, this war is not attested in Oracle bones and thus only mentioned centuries later.

Divination, the chief source of Shang literature, was conducted by several parties: The scribe, the diviner, and a prognosticator, usually the ruler. Scribes would carve the charge into the bone. Some evidence shows scribes being trained through repeatedly carving the 60-day day sexagenery cycle into bones, as well as copying exemplary Oracle bone inscriptions by skilled diviners. Scribes would also come from various places, as shown with Nan 𬆩, who notably wrote with a consistently different word order compared to other scribes even due to his period. Diviners would engage in various rituals, the most well-known being "plastromancy," the burning of a turtle shell until it cracked under the heat. The ruler would then prognosticate as to whether the cracks portray good fortune or not, wherein the cracked bone would be marked to show it was used and/or the result. Bones were sometimes inscribed on again later to note whether the prediction was true or not. Topics within divination were extremely varied; one can find questions about pregnancy, toothache, bad dreams, whether there will be a good harvest, natural disasters, divine protection, whether a war will go well, whether to call an individual to the court, and more. Reports to ancestors also exist, such as records of the "sun being eaten," or what we would call a solar eclipse.

# Use of Literary Chinese

What is extant of Shang literature now is strictly seen in oracle bones, the bones of animals used to carve Han characters during rituals, sacrifices, and divinations. The most common animals used were the Chinese golden thread turtle (Mauremys sinensis) and the now-extinct Short-horned water buffalo (Bubalus mephistopheles). Later on, these bones would be used for record-keeping as well. It is highly likely that the Shang people used other mediums of writing given the maturity of the Han script during this time, but it has not been uncovered, and likely decomposed (e.g. Bamboo slips, leaves, knot-tying, etc). 

The Shang people spoke Old Chinese, and the language was not too far from what is seen in the Zhou-Qin period of the Chinese classics. Structures such as 自……至…… and more can be seen even here.
