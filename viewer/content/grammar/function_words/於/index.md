## Explanation

於 serves the purpose of the English on/at/in, though it can be approximated to "from" and "than" without necessarily implying motion. In the broadest sense, 於 can introduce a place, destination, source, recipient, agent, or point of comparison. For example, this quote from the Analects articulates "Zi Qin asked *to* Zi Gong":
{% corpus_quote path="中國漢文/clean/周朝/東周/春秋時代/魯/論語/論語__學而第一.txt" text="子禽問於子貢曰" highlight="於" source="論語·學而" %}

Therefore, to say a group did something somewhere, a construction like this is possible:
{% corpus_quote path="中國漢文/clean/周朝/東周/春秋時代/魯/論語/論語__八佾第三.txt" text="季氏旅於泰山。" highlight="於" source="論語·八佾" %}

Here, 旅 is the name of a sacrifice: “The Ji clan performed the 旅 sacrifice at Mount Tai.” Elsewhere, 旅 can also concern travel or movement, which makes this sentence easy to misread!

To show the flexibility, 於 can be used with 至 to express "as for", lit. "arrive at":
{% corpus_quote path="中國漢文/clean/周朝/東周/春秋時代/魯/論語/論語__爲政第二.txt" text="子游問孝。子曰：「今之孝者，是謂能養。至於犬馬，皆能有養；不敬，何以別乎？」" highlight="至於" source="論語·爲政" %}

However, 於 cannot normally be used directly with the pronoun sense of 之. Instead, 焉 commonly fills this role and may be understood as “there,” “in it,” “to it,” “from it,” and so on. It is particularly common in the Classic of Mountains and Seas:
{% corpus_quote path="中國漢文/clean/周朝/東周/戰國時代/原不詳/山海經/山經/中山經.txt" text="又北四十里，曰霍山，其木多穀。有獸焉，其狀如貍。" highlight="焉" source="山海經·中山經" %}

Here, 有獸焉 means “there are animals there.”

## Technical explanation

於 commonly functions like a preposition, but syntactically speaking, it is a verb (e.g. Taking subjects and being possible to nominalise with 之). It developed from a stationary or locative verb and came to introduce several kinds of relationship, including location, destination, source, recipient, agent, and comparison.

## Comparisons

* 于 - This character is used as a Simplified Chinese variant in modern Chinese, but they are distinct characters in Literary Chinese. 于 typically means "to go". In Simplified Chinese editions of classical texts from the People's Republic of China, these characters are commonly merged.
* 乎 - This can be used to express an unstressed form of 於.

## Patterns & Binomes

* X 之於 Y - "X in relation to Y" or "as for X with regard to Y." An idiomatic expression used to introduce a topic.
* 於是 - "Thereupon," "at this point," or "in these circumstances."
